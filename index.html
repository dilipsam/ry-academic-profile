About Me

Academic

My journey as an academic began with social work education at TISS and has grown through teaching, research, curriculum development and academic leadership. I enjoy creating learning spaces where students can connect ideas with lived realities, ask difficult questions and learn through experience. Over the years, I have also found myself increasingly drawn to building programmes and academic spaces that make education more relevant, participatory and socially meaningful.

Practitioner

My formative years with grassroots organisations shaped the way I understand social change. Working closely with communities and organisations taught me that good ideas matter only when they can respond to real needs and translate into meaningful action. My work has included project design, monitoring and evaluation, social impact consulting, organisational development and resource mobilisation. These experiences continue to influence how I approach research, teaching and collaboration.

Academic-Practitioner

I see myself as an academic-practitioner (pracademic)—someone who moves between the classroom, the field and research. The field gives me questions, research helps me make sense of them, and teaching gives me an opportunity to share and rethink that learning with others. I try to bring field realities into the classroom and take academic knowledge back into practice, while creating spaces where students, practitioners and communities can learn from one another. For me, this connection between knowledge, practice and people is what gives my work its meaning.

Field Action Projects

Anubhav Mumbai

Anubhav Mumbai is a youth development initiative that uses a cascade development methodology to create change. The approach builds the capacity of a core group of young people, who in turn engage and support others, creating a multiplier effect that extends beyond individual participants. The initiative connects youth leadership, experiential learning, peer engagement and community action, creating pathways for young people to become active participants in their own development and in creating positive change within their communities.

Cell for Advancement of Social Work

The Cell for Advancement of Social Work focused on exploring and strengthening the professional position of social work in India. Its work examined areas of advocacy, professional recognition and the evolving role of social work across institutions and sectors. The initiative sought to create greater visibility for the profession and contribute to conversations that influence the recognition, development and future direction of social work in India.

Saksham

Through Saksham, the focus has been on strengthening the capacities of frontline professionals and systems working with vulnerable populations. This included work with evaluators and stakeholders involved in HIV Targeted Intervention programmes, as well as capacity building of counsellors under the National TB Programme. The work emphasised training, technical support, capacity development and practice strengthening, enabling frontline professionals to respond more effectively to complex public health and social challenges.
